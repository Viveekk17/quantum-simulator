Adaptive Suspension & Quantum-Circuit Control

Two-engine simulator · Vehicle Dynamics + Quantum Computing

A real quarter-car MR-damper model, and a real one-qubit circuit — shown side by side, never conflated.

This prototype runs two independent, physically honest simulations. Vehicle Physics solves the actual quarter-car equations of motion for a magnetorheological (MR) damper with a numerical ODE integrator. Quantum Circuit builds and runs a genuine single-qubit circuit — real 2×2 unitary matrices, Bloch-sphere state, Born-rule measurement — whose measured bit is then fed to the vehicle controller as a true random seed. Nothing here pretends the suspension itself is a quantum device.

▸ Currently simulating: quarter-car ODE (RK4) — 4 state variables, Bingham-plastic MR force law, solved 500× per second.

msẍ₁ = −ks(x₁−x₂) − FMR
muẍ₂ = ks(x₁−x₂) + FMR − kt(x₂−xr) − ct(ẋ₂−ẋr)

Standard quarter-car 2-DOF model. x₁ = sprung mass (body) displacement, x₂ = unsprung mass (wheel) displacement, xr = road input. FMR is a simplified Bingham-plastic MR force law (Spencer et al. 1997) — a viscous term plus a field-controlled yield-stress term. Integrated with classical 4th-order Runge–Kutta (Kutta, 1901) at dt = 2 ms.

▸ Why the controller isn't "stiffer = always safer"

The sprung-mass natural frequency is fn = (1/2π)·√(ks/ms) ≈ 1.2 Hz. Below/near fn (low-speed potholes, large slow undulations), stiffening reduces body excursion. Above fn (most real road texture at normal speed), stiffening amplifies transmitted acceleration — softening is the comfort-correct choice. This was verified by sweeping RMS body acceleration across excitation frequencies for both extremes before this controller was written (see console derivation notes).

Comfort metric follows ISO 2631-1 whole-body-vibration weighting convention (RMS body acceleration).

Each cycle samples a soft/stiff action using the controller's probabilities — over many cycles the split converges to P(soften)/P(stiffen) above.

Circuit → Controller Bridge

honest coupling

The measured qubit bit does not replace the physics. It is used the one way a toy circuit can honestly touch a classical controller: as a true random seed for the stochastic road-noise term in the quarter-car model, and as a tie-breaker when the controller's stiff/soft confidence is near 50/50. This is analogous to real proposals for quantum-random-number-generator (QRNG) seeding in control systems — a legitimate, narrow role, not a claim that suspension damping runs on qubits.
